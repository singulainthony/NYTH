Make Your Phone Easier to See and Hear: Accessibility Settings for North York Seniors

Many seniors assume that struggling to read a phone screen is simply part of getting older, and that nothing can be done short of buying a new device. That is not true. Every iPhone and Android phone sold today has a set of built-in settings designed exactly for this problem — and they are free, permanent, and set up once.

This guide covers the settings that make the biggest difference for the least effort. Everything here uses the phone you already own.

The right accessibility settings make every screen comfortable to use.

Make the Text Bigger and Bolder (iPhone)

Start here. This single change improves almost everything else.

  1. Open Settings, then tap Accessibility.
  2. Tap Display & Text Size.
  3. Tap Larger Text and drag the slider to the right. A preview appears as you drag.
  4. Go back and turn on Bold Text — it thickens every letter and helps a great deal with light-coloured type.
  5. Turn on Increase Contrast if grey text on white still looks faint.

For an even larger effect, go to Settings > Display & Brightness > Display Zoom and choose Larger Text. This makes everything on screen bigger, not just text, and is the closest thing to a "make my phone simpler" switch.

Pro Tip: You can change text size in one app at a time without touching the rest of the phone. Open the app, then add Text Size to Control Center (Settings > Control Center) and adjust the slider whenever you need to.

Make the Text Bigger (Android)

  1. Open Settings, then Display.
  2. Tap Font size and drag the slider to the largest comfortable setting.
  3. Then tap Display size if you want everything on screen — icons, buttons, and text — to grow as well.
  4. Turn on Bold text under Display for extra legibility.

Make the Phone Easier to Hear

Hearing difficulty on a phone usually has less to do with volume than with clarity. These settings help:

  • Call audio clarity (iPhone): Settings > Accessibility > Audio & Visual > Headphone Accommodations and enable Phone Noise Cancellation, which cuts background hiss on calls.
  • Mono Audio: If you hear better in one ear, turn on Mono Audio so both channels play through both earbuds instead of splitting the sound.
  • Sound Recognition: Your phone can listen for a doorbell, a smoke alarm, or a running tap and alert you on screen. Choose which sounds matter to you.
  • Hearing aids: Many modern hearing aids work directly with iPhone. If yours are "Made for iPhone," pairing them in Settings > Accessibility > Hearing Devices will route calls straight to them.
  • Live Listen: With AirPods or compatible hearing aids, your phone can act as a remote microphone — useful when placed in front of a speaker at a meeting.

On Android, look under Settings > Accessibility for Sound Amplifier, Live Transcribe, and Hearing aid compatibility. Live Transcribe in particular is remarkable: it shows a live written transcript of what someone is saying to you.

Magnifier and Spoken Content

Two features change how you interact with small print entirely:

  • Magnifier turns your phone into a digital magnifying glass with a light. It is invaluable for pill bottles, bank statements, and restaurant menus. Enable it under Accessibility > Magnifier, then open it by triple-clicking the side button.
  • Spoken Content (Accessibility > Spoken Content) makes the phone read on-screen text aloud. Turn on Speak Selection and any text you highlight can be read to you — a huge help for long emails.

Magnifier and spoken content turn small print into something you can comfortably read.

Touch Helps, Not Just Sight and Sound

If your hands are stiff, or if the phone sometimes does not register a tap, these settings reduce the frustration:

  • AssistiveTouch puts a floating button on screen for actions that normally need a swipe or a squeeze.
  • Touch Accommodations makes the screen wait longer for your tap, and ignores accidental double taps.
  • Back Tap lets you double-tap the back of the phone to trigger an action, such as opening the Magnifier.
  • Reachability brings the top of the screen down so you can reach buttons one-handed.

Set It Up Once, Then Forget It

The reason many seniors never benefit from these settings is simply that nobody sat down and turned them on. It takes about forty-five minutes to go through them properly, comparing options side by side and keeping what helps. After that, the improvement is permanent, and nothing needs to be redone.

Final Thoughts

Needing larger text is not a reason to give up your phone — it is a reason to spend five minutes in Settings. Start with Larger Text and Bold Text today. That one change alone makes messaging, email, and reading the news considerably easier.

Everything in this guide is already built into the phone you own. Nothing needs to be purchased, and nothing you change can break the device.
